How this experiment works

Bridges are modeled as simple 2D trusses across a fixed 8-toothpick span. The genome shifts and raises the ten upper joints, changing triangle proportions while the 39 connected members remain in place. Every member segment counts as one whole toothpick, even when it is shorter than one; the design stays within a 40-toothpick budget. The solver uses each segment’s actual length for force and buckling estimates.

The glue control uses a provisional white-PVA/Elmer’s-style setting relative to the modeled wood strength. Its low-end default is adapted from a 0.4-inch lap-joint test that reports an overall range across five glue products, not an Elmer’s-specific result. The model assumes identical lap area at every member end, axial tension as bond demand, and direct wood bearing in compression; it does not simulate peel, actual glue area, surface preparation, cure, or interacting multi-stick joints.

This is an educational visualization, not an engineering tool. The displayed kg-equivalent loads use illustrative strength assumptions, not measured toothpick data. Real toothpicks, joints, glue, materials, and three-dimensional bracing behave differently from this simplified model; never use these results to design or certify a real structure.
